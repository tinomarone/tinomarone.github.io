Private equity and hospital acquisitions

Research assistant · Prof. Ryan McDevitt · 2026–present

Led by a team of graduate students and economics faculty from Duke University, the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, the University of California, Berkeley, Indiana University, Ohio State University, and Washington University in St. Louis.

I work with messy administrative data to corroborate and strengthen existing commercial databases on the role of private equity in healthcare. The administrative sources range from PECOS to CCN/AHA and HCRIS. The analysis is mostly in Python (pandas, rapidfuzz), and I also incorporate LLM-assisted extraction and validation.

Medicare durable medical equipment fraud

Research assistant · Prof. Ryan McDevitt · NBER Working Paper 34802 · 2026–present

Diwan, Eliason, League, Leder-Luis, McDevitt & Roberts. I work with a PhD student at Boston University and with Ryan McDevitt at Washington University in St. Louis.

I turn enforcement documents into a structured panel, resolving supplier identifiers across sources and scoring how much each piece of evidence should be trusted depending on whether it was court-verified or circumstantial.

Extraction from unstructured text is never fully reliable. The confidence score exists so downstream users can see where the uncertainty sits instead of inheriting it silently.

Market outcomes in non-competitive-bidding areas

Independent seminar paper · 2026

Written with the guidance of Prof. Molly Moore of Boston College, formerly at Washington University in St. Louis.

An analysis of Medicare DMEPOS markets outside the initial competitive bidding program, asking what happens to prices, claims, and supplier participation in areas the program reached synthetically. I study the fifteen most rural states to understand how market outcomes there differ from those dominated by metropolitan areas.

All fifteen states were treated at once, so there is no control group. The paper argues that this limits what can be identified, but that the rural subset is worth examining precisely because the literature on competitive bidding tends to overlook it.

Value factor construction

In progress · 2026

Rebuilding the standard and timely-price versions of the HML value factor from public data, to see for myself how a lagged-price convention changes the factor's correlation with momentum.

A replication is only as good as its handling of look-ahead bias. Notes on what I got wrong first will go in the repository alongside the code.

Completed individually, with advising from Dr. Elisabetta Pastori.

An investigation of convergence for a family of constants generalizing γ, drawing on analytic number theory, calculus, and analysis. It builds on Tom Apostol's work on asymptotic expansions.
